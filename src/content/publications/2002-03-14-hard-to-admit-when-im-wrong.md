Hard to admit when I'm wrong. I'd like to pretend that the difficulty comes from having to admit being wrong rather than from having been right too often. Ego is gratifying and people enjoy urging your guns. Ayn Rand wrote that she was not an advocate of capitalism but was concerned about the problems surrounding anyone trying to debate at length. People are willing to satisfy their emotions. You notice you work hard. You deserve it. Well, she was right on when she said that pride only hurts. The reason is that a much more satisfying feeling than emotion is learning.

We in technical fields think the folks in authority are mostly rational and objective, but we also look at the few disagreements and debates: Unix versus Windows, C versus Java. Every side has its advocates. The debate is often about allegiance rather than evidence. In the end, what matters is that people think for themselves and understand that self-minded individuals gain their own arguments behind top-down institutions. Expertise is valuable, but it cannot replace curiosity.

I have had to admit that my earlier views about technology and software were incomplete. Microsoft, operating systems, and free software all have strengths and weaknesses. Open source is not automatically perfect and proprietary software is not automatically evil. The useful question is what works, what can be inspected, and what helps people solve problems.
