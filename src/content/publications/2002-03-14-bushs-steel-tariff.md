One problem is that our government is busily surrendering little pieces of our right of self-government—to antiglobal organizations such as the UN and the World Trade Organization. Various European countries threatened to file charges against us if President Bush's decision may not endure. The tariff could set off a trade war where countries put up their tariffs on everyone else's goods in retaliation, meaning higher prices for consumers.

Slapping on a thirty-percent tariff is not conservative: conservatives, at least when they are campaigning, repeatedly insist that the government should not interfere with the market. This tariff isn't compassionate either because the higher prices that result will have the most significant impact on people of low income and the folks on fixed incomes such as our grandparents collecting Social Security.

Bush's new tariff amounts to a bailout of the steel industry. In the time leading up to the tariff, we heard a lot about the plight of the American steel industry. Maybe it is only coincidental that steel states include the economies in several politically important states such as Pennsylvania and Ohio. Where do we draw the line? Which industries get the special treatment and which are left to tough it out?

Again, this market interference is not conservative. Further, coming to the rescue of these industries gives the strong impression that the president sacrificed principle for political expediency. It is not compassionate either. Rather than leave people to pursue their own self-interest, the tariff encourages people to stay in an industry that does not have much of a future.

Fleischer spins it as giving the steel industry time to adjust, but no one will adjust; they will ask for more and more federal protection. I can write software, and that means that I will be paid less and less valuable as I age. That doesn't mean that government owes me something. Instead, I have a responsibility to develop new skills and save money while times are good. Government-created illusions that everyone will always be able to find work at a former salary will hurt, not help. Being part of the economy is not a free ride.

The president has a degree in history, but he didn't study much American history or the events and arguments that led to the Great Depression. We are suffering an economic hangover from the massive Internet build-out of the 90s, just as the country suffered from the massive railroad build-out in the previous century. Hoover imposed enormous protectionist tariffs.

The Democrats are threatening to raise taxes, and state and local taxes are certain to rise because most states are faced with the big slowdown. Tax hikes in 1930 damaged the US economy and pushed us toward the Depression. We don't have Hoover's wage controls that artificially inflated prices and destroyed jobs, but that is hardly a reason to repeat the mistake.
