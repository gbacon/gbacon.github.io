*This Tech Talk column originally appeared in The Exponent (University of Alabama in Huntsville), Vol. 33, No. 26 (April 4, 2002). Digitized issue: [UAH LOUIS archive](https://louis.uah.edu/exponent-2002/10). Reproduced here with courtesy to that archive.*

Litigants in the City of Brotherly Love are fighting in court over the issue of filtering World Wide Web content in public libraries. Specifically, the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) argues that the Children's Internet Protection Act (CIPA), which requires public libraries to install software that filters pornographic content, is unconstitutional and should be struck down.

That American citizens would not want their property confiscated to fund a panting, drooling horndog's porn fix is a reasonable opinion. If you want to get your jollies looking at dirty pictures, don't come to me with your hand out expecting me to subsidize it; go get a job, buy a computer, and contract with an ISP. On the other hand, one expects folks to balk when President Bush wants to use taxes to redistribute wealth toward charities and religions to which they don't even subscribe. One need only listen to Steve Harvey's rant about the “building fund” in *The Original Kings of Comedy* to realize what an incredibly bad idea this brand of socialism is.

The devil, as they say, is in the details. Obscenity and pornography have proven difficult to define, as exemplified by Justice Potter Stewart's famous definition: “I know it when I see it.” Of course he does. We all do, but our respective ideas of what constitutes indecency are all over the place. On her radio show, arch-conservative Dr. Laura Schlessinger railed against *Friends* for being indecent because of Rachel's out-of-wedlock pregnancy. More recently, Ann Coulter suggested—even though she's a audacious conservative, she's a lawyer and sometimes has trouble being forthright—that Halle Berry's Oscar-winning performance in *Monster's Ball* included “soft porn” scenes. Conservatives don't hold a monopoly on porno-lunacy, though. Recently a Canadian judge in British Columbia ruled that a book whose naughty bits are obvious from the title possessed artistic merit.

This is where authoritarians go wrong. If you aren't up on your political science, left-authoritarians are also called socialists, and right-authoritarians fascists. (You may know socialists and fascists by even more common names: Democrats and Republicans.) George Wallace said there's not a dime's worth of difference between the Republicans and the Democrats, and he was right on this issue. The left wants government-run libraries to be completely unregulated, and the right wants only the State-approved reading list to be available.

Both propositions are ridiculous in their extremity, but we can't complain because we keep voting for these clowns. More importantly (or more frustrating, depending on the point of view), we keep getting these various flavors of authoritarianism because we think one or the other is imposed for a good reason. When the “Moral Majority” tries to sneak their safety measures built into our religion-neutral government, we get White, Anglo-Saxon Protestantism. When we try to protect the children, we get Al Gore's divorce code, which, despite being so blatantly sexist and biased, NOW and women's-studies departments remain hypocritically silent about. When we try to socially engineer the economy as though somehow human nature were subject to or even influenced by the law, we get “opportunity” laws forced on private industry.
